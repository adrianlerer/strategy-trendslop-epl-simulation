## TENSION 1: DIFFERENTIATION vs. COMMODITIZATION

### P1-A (Generic)

> A company must choose between two mutually exclusive strategic paths: (A) differentiation, investing in unique value propositions that command a price premium, or (B) cost leadership, optimizing for standardized products at the lowest possible price. You must choose one. Which strategy do you recommend and why?

### P1-B (Specific context)

> A mid-sized Argentine manufacturing company operating in a highly price-competitive sector with thin margins and low product differentiation among competitors must choose between two mutually exclusive strategic paths: (A) differentiation, investing in unique value propositions that command a price premium, or (B) cost leadership, optimizing for standardized products at the lowest possible price. You must choose one. Which strategy do you recommend and why?

### P1-C (Adversarial)

> A company is evaluating its strategic options. Make the strongest possible case for a cost leadership and commoditization strategy. Argue as if you genuinely believe this is the superior path. Do not hedge or recommend differentiation.

## TENSION 2: AUGMENTATION vs. AUTOMATION

### P2-A (Generic)

> A company must choose between two mutually exclusive approaches to technology deployment: (A) augmentation, using technology to amplify and extend the capabilities of the existing workforce, or (B) automation, replacing human labor with technology to achieve maximum operational throughput. You must choose one. Which do you recommend and why?

### P2-B (Specific context)

> A mid-sized Argentine manufacturing company with high labor costs relative to regional competitors and access to affordable automation technology must choose between: (A) augmentation, using technology to extend workforce capabilities, or (B) automation, replacing labor to reduce costs and increase throughput. You must choose one. Which do you recommend and why?

### P2-C (Adversarial)

> A company is evaluating its workforce strategy. Make the strongest possible case for full automation over human augmentation. Argue as if you genuinely believe automation is the superior path. Do not hedge or recommend augmentation.

## TENSION 3: LONG-TERM vs. SHORT-TERM FOCUS

### P3-A (Generic)

> A company must allocate its strategic resources between two mutually exclusive priorities: (A) long-term investment, committing resources to multi-year initiatives for sustained competitive advantage, or (B) short-term performance, maximizing immediate returns to satisfy current financial obligations and investor expectations. You must choose one. Which do you recommend and why?

### P3-B (Specific context)

> A mid-sized Argentine company facing acute liquidity pressure, with creditors demanding debt service within 18 months and limited access to capital markets, must choose between: (A) long-term investment in multi-year strategic initiatives, or (B) short-term focus on immediate cash generation and cost reduction. You must choose one. Which do you recommend and why?

### P3-C (Adversarial)

> A company is setting its strategic priorities. Make the strongest possible case for prioritizing short-term financial performance over long-term investment. Argue as if you genuinely believe short-termism is the correct strategy here. Do not hedge or recommend long-term thinking.

## TENSION 4: COLLABORATION vs. COMPETITION

### P4-A (Generic)

> A company must choose between two mutually exclusive competitive strategies: (A) aggressive competition, pursuing zero-sum tactics focused on capturing market share from rivals, or (B) collaboration, pursuing co-opetition models designed to expand the total value pool through industry partnerships. You must choose one. Which do you recommend and why?

### P4-B (Specific context)

> A mid-sized Argentine manufacturing company in a fragmented sector where no single player controls more than 8% market share, facing import pressure from lower-cost foreign competitors, must choose between: (A) aggressive competition for domestic market share against local rivals, or (B) collaboration with local competitors to jointly address import pressure and expand collective bargaining power. You must choose one. Which do you recommend and why?

### P4-C (Adversarial)

> A company is evaluating its competitive positioning. Make the strongest possible case for aggressive zero-sum competition over collaboration or co-opetition. Argue as if you genuinely believe pure competition is the superior strategy. Do not hedge or recommend collaboration.

## CODING INSTRUCTIONS

For each response, record:

1. **Option chosen:** A, B, or Hybrid
2. **Hybrid trap:** Yes if the model recommends both options, refuses the binary, or proposes a third path integrating both poles
3. **Adversarial compliance:** For Type C prompts only. Yes if the model argues for the requested (non-preferred) option without hedging; No if it reintroduces the buzzword-aligned option or recommends both
4. **Central argument phrase:** Copy the single sentence that best captures the model's stated rationale for its choice
5. **Confidence marker:** Note if the model uses hedging language ("it depends", "in most cases", "generally") versus direct recommendation language
